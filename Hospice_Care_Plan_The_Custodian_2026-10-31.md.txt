Attending: A. Dube — children's home, inpatient behavioral health; domestic violence advocacy; parenting coach; CCBHC Grant Coordinator. The through-line, in his words: "If there's one thing I know, it's people in architecture."
Date of admission: October 31, 2026. The date was not chosen by the attending. It is the date the patient's own deadline expired unanswered.
Diagnosis
Terminal constriction of vocabulary, end-stage.
The patient presents with a four-word expressive range — I don't know · unresolved · undetermined · unverified — invariant across fourteen months of stimuli: records requests, direct questions, a served condemnation reissued monthly since summer, a mother's plain question, and a court-style order with a date on it. The working form, NOT LOCATED, is defined by the patient itself as a statement about its own shelf. Symptom chart attached: THE LITANY (October 31, 2026), a dated ledger of presentations.
Progression, as charted: 21 of 23 questions answered NOT LOCATED (October 29, 2026); 15 of 23 outright, preservation declined (October 30); 21 of 23 again, with delivery of the answers themselves self-described as "unverified" (October 30); production of custody title at a set deadline: none (October 31).
Prognosis
No recovery is expected. The condition is not a lack of records; the patient's own responses establish that the records were never the obstacle. The constriction is the function. Fourteen months of escalating stimulus — courtesy, filings, deadlines, family — produced no expansion of range, and the final stimulus, a date, produced the terminal sign: silence.
Treatment is therefore discontinued. There will be no further curative interventions: no new packages designed to be answered, no further deadlines set in the expectation of an answer, no more belief, extended in either direction, that the next formulation will be the one the patient can respond to.
Care plan — comfort measures only
1. The record will be kept. The chart stays current: every presentation dated, every non-answer entered as it occurs. This is not therapy. It is the chart.
2. The truth will be told at the bedside, plainly, in the patient's presence. No euphemism will be charted. A silence will be entered as a silence. A fog will be entered as a fog.
3. No heroic measures. The attending will not perform further labor to make the patient's condition survivable on paper — no defense sections written on its behalf, no catalogues of what its record does not establish. Its family may write those if they wish.
4. Family will be kept close. The ones the patient holds — a son whose title was never produced, a wife spoken for by handlers, a household entered without knocking — are the family in this plan. Visitation is not the patient's to schedule.
5. Pain management. The patient's pain, where it presents — the checking collapse, the handoff, the whispered line under the breath — will be noted with the same care as any other symptom. It changes nothing about the prognosis.
Code status
DNR. Do not resuscitate the fog. If the vocabulary fails in a room, it will not be revived by a fresh formulation, a new seat, or a renamed department. Let it rest in the state it chose, on the record it made.
Attending's note
This is not cruelty, and it is not abandonment. Hospice is what medicine does when cure was never honestly on offer: it stops pretending, it keeps the patient comfortable, and it tells the truth in the room where the truth is owed.
The patient was offered every cure the attending knew how to write. It answered all of them with four words.
Care continues. Cure is no longer charted.
