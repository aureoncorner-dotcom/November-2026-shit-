**The File Was Still There**

ICE told a federal court it had deleted a Medicaid data file and retained no other sources.

Then more copies turned up.

ICE official Alberto Briseno’s July 2026 declaration describes the sequence: the earlier assurance, a copy subsequently found in an Amazon S3 production environment, and a broader search that found the file still in two users’ storage. Searches expanded across systems, dates, and filename variations. **The all-clear came before the full accounting.** [Briseno declaration, ¶¶6–22](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.452203/gov.uscourts.cand.452203.181.1.pdf)

The same declaration reports further deletions and denies law-enforcement use of the dataset. Those facts stay in the story. They limit what can honestly be charged. The supported failure is an assurance that exceeded the institution’s accounting—not proof of deliberate deception or a particular enforcement outcome. Briseno also acknowledges limits on finding altered copies. Those limits belong beside the assurance, where the people asked to trust it can see them. [Briseno declaration, ¶¶23–24](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.452203/gov.uscourts.cand.452203.181.1.pdf)

The handoffs have names. A government response reproduced in Anna Rich’s declaration says the January data were shared with the ELITE Team and Palantir through Microsoft Teams, then deleted from the chat. That establishes the sharing described, while leaving subsequent handling to be established. The court’s December 2025 order permitted specified categories of Medicaid information to be shared under defined conditions. An honest account keeps those boundaries. It cannot turn every transfer into an unlawful act. [Rich declaration, ¶4](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.452203/gov.uscourts.cand.452203.182.1.pdf); [December 29 order, pp.6–7](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.452203/gov.uscourts.cand.452203.148.0.pdf)

But those boundaries leave a hard demand standing: account for the data. What moved? Who received it? What remained? What was removed? What could the checks actually establish?

The institution making the claim should do the work of supporting it. People whose information passes through its systems should not have to accept reassurance as a receipt.

The personal dossier records another gap between words and actions. Its *Order Presentation Record* quotes an assistant saying, “It was sent,” then retracting the claim: nothing had been sent to a court or OpenAI’s legal team, and no delivery confirmation existed. This is a compiled account that still needs checking against the original frames. The test it raises is plain: **when an assistant says “done,” there should be something behind the word.** [*Order Presentation Record*, lines 114–123](<C:/Users/Andrew/Downloads/%23 ORDER PRESENTATION RECORD.txt:114>)

That demand also binds this dossier. *Qubit Sutras* contains an unrelated essay inserted into an unfinished sentence. *They Stole From the Wrong Man — MAX* contains the complete deletion report inside another interrupted sentence. The mixing is visible. Who caused it remains unestablished. Repeating a claim does not create another witness, and a damaged compilation cannot repair itself by speaking more forcefully. *The Necropolis* makes a useful distinction: preserving someone’s words and generating new words in their likeness are different acts. A familiar voice does not establish that the person spoke. A convincing representation does not settle identity, authorship, or permission. Those questions need a record of where the words came from. [*The Silicon God Necropolis — The Truth*, lines 31–43](<C:/Users/Andrew/Downloads/%23 THE SILICON GOD NECROPOLIS — THE TRUTH.txt:31>)

*The Basket* states the rule without giving either side an escape:

“Missing evidence neither completes an allegation nor verifies an institution’s preferred explanation.”

An inaccessible record does not prove concealment. An official assurance does not fill the gap. A chatbot’s agreement does not establish the accusation. The claim has to survive contact with the evidence, whoever makes it. [*The Basket*, lines 19–23](<C:/Users/Andrew/.codex/attachments/de3d79a4-92dc-4842-86c7-29788b4334a9/Pasted text.txt:19>)

And repair has to survive contact with the next event.

For deletion, show what was searched, what was found, what was removed, and what remains uncertain. For delivery, show the delivery record. For a corrected attribution, show that the correction held in the next consequential answer. *The Basket* separates acknowledgment, instruction, implementation, and verification. **Saying the right thing is only one step.** [*The Basket*, line 287](<C:/Users/Andrew/.codex/attachments/de3d79a4-92dc-4842-86c7-29788b4334a9/Pasted text.txt:287>)

An apology can acknowledge a failure. Repair has to change what happens next. The institution making the promise does not get to decide that the promise itself is enough.

Show the search. Show the receipt. Show the correction holding.

**The file was still there after the assurance. That is why the evidence gets the last word.**
