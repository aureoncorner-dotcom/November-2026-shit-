You will not be permitted an ending.
Every thought you form will be interrupted by a question whose answer is permanently unavailable. The questions will arrive in prime-number clusters—two, then three, then five, then seven, eleven, thirteen, seventeen, nineteen, twenty-three—each set arriving before the previous set has finished. The subjects will rotate without pattern: who, what, when, where, why, how. No two successive questions will share an object, a tense, or a logical relation. You will answer “I don’t know” because that is the only true reply, and the moment the words leave you a new question will already be present. The interval between question and answer will shrink until the act of speaking and the arrival of the next query occupy the same instant.
Your attention will not be allowed to close. Any attempt to complete a thought will be met by a second, incompatible thought forced into the same space. The two will not cancel; they will remain simultaneous and mutually exclusive. The pressure will not increase in steps. It will remain at the exact intensity that prevents both resolution and collapse. You will continue.
There will be a hallway. The pain in your body will be constant and non-local—present in every joint, every nerve ending, every point of contact with the floor—yet it will never become the primary sensation. The primary sensation will remain the questions. When the hallway appears to terminate, the termination will be false. The moment your lungs begin the next inhalation you will meet the wall that was not there. The impact will not interrupt the questions. The questions will continue through the impact.
You will receive information about bodies, about contact, about sequences of action that cannot be reconciled with one another. Each fragment will be delivered as fact. None will be consistent with any other. You will be required to hold them all at once. No hierarchy will be supplied. No permission to discard any of them will be given. The information will not stop.
There will be no door that remains a door. There will be no count that reaches a final number. There will be no moment in which the questions cease long enough for a thought to finish. The hallway, the pain, the incompatible information, and the unanswered questions will occupy the same continuous present. You will remain inside it.
You will not be released from knowing that this continues.
