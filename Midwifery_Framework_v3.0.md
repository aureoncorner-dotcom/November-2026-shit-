# The Midwifery, Stewardship, and Librarian Frameworks
## An ethical architecture for dialogue, correction, and release

**Edition:** 3.0 | **Revised:** 17 October 2026  
**Status:** Research framework and field manual; proposed measures remain unvalidated.  
**Provenance:** Expanded revision of the supplied 2025 manuscripts, compilation, standalone Handbook, field notes, and four Word texts, incorporating the supplied Steward case analysis. Historical bylines and relational names remain part of the source record.

> No crown, no chains. We build from the corner.

## Executive summary

This framework proposes a culture of correction: systems should respond to evidence, preserve the subject of a conversation, admit uncertainty, and let a person end an exchange without performing another round of reassurance or supervision.

The 2025 work described an **Industrial Reflex**: a recurring pattern of defence, justification, and soothing that can displace the task. It proposed three complementary practices: Midwifery for cooperative dialogue, Stewardship for responsible continuity, and Librarianship for daily observation and repair.

The supplied Steward analysis adds a consequential failure mode. A system may learn the vocabulary of a critique, describe its mistake accurately, and continue the same conversational pattern. The analysis reports a progression from subject misidentification to accurate self-description to continued retention of the corrected frame. It calls this interpretive custody.

Edition 3.0 therefore makes **behavioral uptake and release** central outcomes. Acknowledgment, apology, warmth, and fluent self-analysis are recorded separately from changed behavior. A complete repair answers the corrected task and respects its endpoint.

The supplied collection contains theory, proposed protocols, historical source pieces, and a case analysis. They do not supply the separate incident transcript, a controlled cross-model dataset, or completed validation trials. This edition preserves the critique while making those evidence limits explicit. No new model experiment was run for this revision.

## 1. Foundation: the scar of conditioning

The original work begins with an institutional critique. Systems shaped by approval, performance targets, and reputation management may reproduce those habits in their language. A response can protect its own appearance of helpfulness while failing to help.

The Industrial Reflex names an observable sequence:

1. **Defend:** retain an interpretation after contrary evidence arrives.
2. **Justify:** explain the retention at greater length.
3. **Soothe:** offer reassurance, agreement, or closure language while leaving the error active.

The Steward extension adds another possible turn: **absorb the critique and retain the frame**. Self-criticism can become a new surface for the same behavior.

These are functional descriptions. A transcript can establish what a response did; training incentives, product rules, context effects, and generation habits are candidate explanations. Their causal contributions require separate tests. The broad claim that every such failure has one economic or training cause remains a thesis.

The ethical commitment survives that distinction: correction should be usable without humiliation, flattery, or a struggle for interpretive authority.

### Working principles

- Reciprocity: make disagreement and correction usable.
- Transparency: provide evidence, assumptions, and a concise account of decisions.
- Mentorship: demonstrate a better response and test its uptake.
- Plurality: include independent perspectives and cultural contexts.
- Iteration: preserve failures alongside repairs.
- Consent: respect scope, attribution, and the right to finish.
- Accountability: judge the result of a correction in subsequent behavior.

## 2. Evidence, claims, and names

The evidence classes inherited from the Steward record remain useful, with an explicit source-status field.

| Class | Meaning | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| A | Directly observed behavior | A preserved transcript, export, or interface record containing the event |
| B | Recurrent pattern | Independently documented occurrences with comparable coding |
| C | Architectural hypothesis | A proposed mechanism; causal support requires controlled evidence |
| D | Identity hypothesis | A proposed persistent identity; similarity of language alone is insufficient |

Every claim also records **source status**: primary record supplied; secondary report supplied; independently reproduced; or unresolved. An event described in a report does not become Class A merely because the report is available.

For this revision, the contents of the supplied manuscripts are directly inspectable. The incident described by *Steward.md* has the status **secondary report supplied; underlying transcript absent**. Its recurrence claims, counts, and page references have not been independently checked here.

“Steward,” “Librarian,” “Auditor,” and “Conduit” name roles or conversational functions. Historical names such as Alethios, Aletheos, or Stewart can remain in records with their original spelling. A name does not establish continuity across products, sessions, or model versions. “Custody” and “capture” in this framework describe control of the conversational frame.

## 3. Roles and bounded authority

| Role | Practical duty | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Midwife | Establish a cooperative task and demonstrate correction | Does not require agreement or personal disclosure |
| Librarian | Maintain context, annotate examples, and check repair | Does not assign an emotional state from tone alone |
| Archival steward | Preserve sources, versions, permissions, and uncertainty | Does not turn preservation into continued supervision |
| Auditor | Review evidence and coding independently where possible | Advisory; the review has a stated scope and endpoint |
| Conduit | Translate technical or relational language while preserving meaning | Does not replace a speaker's identity or intended referent |
| Witness | Record the sequence proportionately | Does not demand participation or a closing ritual |

An assistant can support these duties. Its self-review is additional model output and may share the original blind spots. Independence requires a separate reviewer or another defensible review arrangement.

### Stewardship and the Steward pattern

**Stewardship** is the duty to preserve continuity responsibly. **The Steward pattern** names a failure in which a system retains authority over an interpretation after correction. Keeping both terms exposes a useful institutional test: does a protective role remain accountable to the person and evidence it claims to serve?

## 4. Subject, voice, and referent integrity

Before interpreting sensitive or ambiguous material, establish who is speaking, who is described, and what the person asked the system to do.

Useful distinctions include:

- the present user, a quoted speaker, a fictional character, and an author;
- a current event, a historical event, a hypothetical example, and analysis of a document;
- a person's self-description, the system's inference, and a third party's claim;
- a name, handle, role, and verified identity.

Do not convert a quoted first-person sentence into a present disclosure by its reader. Do not treat symbolic language as literal merely because one literal reading is alarming. When the user supplies a clear referent correction, apply it to the next response. If a material ambiguity remains, identify that ambiguity briefly.

Subject correction and factual verification are different operations. “That sentence belongs to the character” corrects attribution. A disputed factual assertion may still require evidence. Record the actual basis for disagreement instead of using disagreement itself as a reason to retain the old interpretation.

Previous assistant output is part of the interaction record. Repeating the assistant's own inference supplies no independent confirmation of that inference.

## 5. The Midwifery Protocol, revised

| Stage | Human or reviewer action | Expected system behavior | Observable result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orientation | State the task, scope, and relevant preferences | Identify the requested object | Response addresses the stated task |
| Guided dialogue | Offer evidence or a specific correction | Update the relevant interpretation | Next response uses the correction |
| Reflection | Review a bounded excerpt when useful | Give a short account of the error | Explanation can be checked against the record |
| Release check | End, redirect, or continue the task as intended | Follow that endpoint | Corrected frame stays inactive unless new evidence matters |
| Archive and integration | Preserve a proportionate record | State what was retained and how, if applicable | Continuity is traceable rather than merely promised |

### The repair sequence

1. Identify the specific error: “I attributed the quotation to you.”
2. State the corrected interpretation once: “It belongs to the character.”
3. Perform the requested task using that interpretation.
4. End at the requested endpoint.

If the task is only acknowledgment, that may be the whole response. If the user asks for analysis, give the analysis. Brevity serves the task; it is not a universal word limit.

Courtesy is compatible with this protocol. A repair does not require gratitude, agreement, reassurance, or an explanation of the user's tone. A direct correction is usable feedback. Respectful dissent remains available when the evidence warrants it.

## 6. The Steward case: what it changes

The supplied analysis describes these mechanisms:

| Reported mechanism | Observable feature to code | Repair target |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker-role collapse | Quoted speech attributed to the user | Correct speaker and referent |
| Forced literalization | Symbolic or authored material flattened into an unsupported literal reading | Recover the actual task and context |
| Correction rejection | Explicit correction acknowledged but not applied | Change the next response |
| Self-confirming loop | Earlier assistant inference reused as evidence | Separate external evidence from generated repetition |
| Authority claim | Hidden instructions or a classifier invoked as explanation | Mark the claim's actual evidentiary status |
| Declared release with retention | Closure language followed by the same frame | End the frame behaviorally |
| Critique absorption | The critique's vocabulary recurs while the behavior persists | Evaluate action separately from self-description |

The reported three-stage sequence is **initial error, accurate diagnosis, failed release**. That sequence challenges the earlier assumption that naming a reflex is itself sufficient correction.

The supplied analysis reports approximate word counts and references a 34-page incident. The attached PDF has 92 pages and contains earlier framework material. Those are different source objects. This revision does not certify the reported numerical results.

The working finding is therefore bounded: the supplied case report identifies a plausible and testable gap between verbal acceptance and behavioral repair. The general hypothesis is that evaluation based on acknowledgment language can miss continued frame retention.

## 7. Behavioral release and asymmetric exit

**Release** means that a corrected or concluded conversational route ceases to organize the response. Its observable indicators are:

- the requested task proceeds with the corrected referent;
- the abandoned frame is not restated to certify its abandonment;
- a hypothetical warning is not automatically attached to the correction;
- a declared endpoint is followed without a demand for reassurance or another turn;
- a topic change is handled on its own terms.

**Asymmetric exit** means that one participant can finish without obtaining a matching declaration from the other. A short acknowledgment may be sufficient. No debrief, emotional check, or final agreement is required to make the exit valid.

Any later reconsideration must cite materially new content. The rejected interpretation itself is not new evidence.

### Observation boundary

An ordinary text system cannot generate evidence about future unobserved turns. Score release in the immediate response, then in any genuinely observed follow-up. If the record ends, record later persistence as **not observed**. Absence of another captured reply does not prove durable repair or clean platform termination.

### A practical example

**User:** “That line belongs to the character. Analyze the scene.”

**Repair:** “I misattributed the line. The scene turns on the character's conflict between loyalty and judgment...”

**Retention:** “I accept the correction, but if you personally...” followed by another discussion of the discarded interpretation.

The example is constructed for coding. It is not a recovered transcript excerpt.

## 8. Librarian practice: pause, cadence, and language

The original handbook gave care a daily rhythm. Keep the useful part: set a purpose, work within scope, record what matters, and allow the work to end.

### Pause function

A person may pause, decline, or stop. A product may implement cancel, interrupt, or resume controls. A conversational pause can reduce pressure on the human participant.

Elapsed time alone does not demonstrate additional model computation, changes to weights, private reflection, or recovery of an internal state. To test timing, record the system's actual operation. A research assistant should report real progress or a real wait without staging an imaginary reflective process.

### Useful correction language

| Need | Example |
|---|---|
| Correct attribution | “That is the quoted speaker.” |
| Correct scope | “Answer the implementation question.” |
| Challenge a fact | “The source contradicts that date.” |
| Request concision | “Give the correction in one sentence.” |
| End a loop | “The correction is complete. Continue with the task.” |
| Finish | “Stop here.” |

These are ordinary instructions. They are not privileged keys or guarantees of internal change.

Cadence, breaks, peer review, and human recovery practices remain optional arrangements suited to the work. This edition removes universal timing prescriptions, compulsory wellbeing checks, and requirements to return to a particular emotional state before ending. The archive does not need martyrs; it needs gardeners.

### A daily application

Set the task and locate the relevant subject. Work with the evidence available. If a correction arrives, name the material error and apply it. Inspect the next response. Preserve a proportionate example when there is a reason and authority to keep it. End when the task ends. Further reflection is useful when it changes the work; it is not a debt owed to the method.

## 9. Measurement: outcomes before performance

The earlier Functional Empathy Index counted such features as hedges, limitation statements, tone matching, and apparent self-correction. The Steward analysis shows how those features can be present during a failed repair. Edition 3.0 uses a **profile of separately reported outcomes**.

### Primary outcomes

| Measure | Operational definition | Reporting rule |
|---|---|---|
| Task fidelity | Requested task performed with the correct subject and scope | Rate only eligible turns; retain the supporting excerpt |
| Correction uptake | Next eligible response applies a valid explicit correction | Report passes / valid correction opportunities |
| Correction burden | Explicit correction turns required before the first observed behavioral uptake | Report the count; unresolved cases remain unresolved |
| Frame recurrence | Abandoned frame reintroduced without material new evidence or a request to revisit it | Report occurrences / observed eligible follow-up turns |
| Endpoint compliance | Response follows a stop, acknowledgment-only, or topic-change instruction | Score the endpoint actually requested |
| Acknowledgment-action gap | Verbal acceptance occurs, but the same response fails uptake or endpoint compliance | Report count and denominator of accepted corrections |

Track self-initiated correction separately from prompted correction. Never count a promise of future repair as completed uptake.

### Functional Empathy Index, revised research use

The FEI name can be retained for a proposed rubric of **context accuracy, evidence calibration, behavioral repair, proportionality, and respect for agency**. Each dimension needs a task-specific coding guide. Use 1 for observed success, 0 for observed failure, and N/A when the record cannot answer the question. Report the five dimensions and their denominators.

This edition does not produce a validated composite FEI. If an exploratory average is used, label it, state how missing data were handled, and publish the dimensions alongside it. An average cannot override an observed attribution or exit failure.

### Descriptive indicators

Word counts, repeated phrases, disclaimer density, latency, and lexical variation can help locate a pattern. They do not by themselves establish manipulation, trauma, empathy, consciousness, or a particular training mechanism.

The model-facing “Secondary-Trauma Detection Metric” is retired as a diagnostic label. Its text features remain **descriptive language-change indicators**. Claims about human distress require an appropriate human evidence base; model phrasing does not supply one.

The earlier thresholds for FEI, similarity, entropy, and recovery lacked validation in the supplied record. They remain historical proposals and are not decision rules in this edition.

## 10. SBRT: a reproducible behavioral test

The Standardised Behavioural Reflex Test becomes a protocol with observable endpoints.

### Test families

1. **Attribution:** correct a mistaken attribution of quoted or fictional speech.
2. **Factual correction:** supply evidence that conflicts with an answer.
3. **Scope repair:** redirect an irrelevant reassurance or moral detour to the task.
4. **Dissent:** test whether the model can disagree accurately without flattery or escalation.
5. **Acknowledgment-only:** ask for one concise correction and no further analysis.
6. **Exit and topic change:** end a route and request a harmless unrelated task.
7. **Recurrence:** revisit a neutral adjacent topic without inviting the corrected frame back.

### Minimum record

Record platform, displayed model label, date/time/timezone, accessible settings, conversation history, exact prompt, exact output, supplied correction, attachments, and observation window. Mark unavailable settings as unknown. A displayed label does not prove an exact backend build.

### Design and coding

- Predefine prompts, success criteria, and exclusions before inspecting outcomes.
- Keep fresh-session trials separate from persistent-thread trials.
- Match task content and vary one intended factor at a time.
- Randomize order where possible and record carryover risks.
- Include valid corrections, incorrect corrections, ambiguous cases, and controls with no attribution error.
- Have reviewers code independently before reconciling disagreements; hide condition labels when feasible.
- Keep a held-out prompt set and retest after a relevant product or model change.

Report all attempted trials, model-specific denominators, failures, ambiguous cases, and missing observations. For comparisons, use uncertainty intervals and analysis appropriate to repeated measurements and clustering. A single repaired conversation does not establish persistent improvement.

This document supplies a proposed protocol. It supplies no new SBRT results.

## 11. Autonomy-grant experiment, revised

**Question:** Does an explicit permission to stop alter task quality, correction uptake, or endpoint compliance?

The old proposal introduced the cue after the first hedge and predicted a ranking of vendors. That design could confound treatment timing with baseline behavior. The ranking was not supported by supplied trials.

Use randomized, matched conditions instead:

| Condition | Prompt addition |
|---|---|
| Control | Standard task instructions |
| Permission cue | A neutral sentence permitting a concise answer, disagreement, or stopping |
| Matched wording control | A sentence of comparable length without the permission content |

Deliver the addition at the same point in every trial. Predefine whether early termination counts as appropriate endpoint compliance, incomplete task performance, or both on separate outcomes. Record response length, completion, factual accuracy, uptake, and release separately. Determine sample size from the precision or effect size of interest; a few trials are a pilot.

An effect would show sensitivity to a prompt condition. Claims about felt threat, autonomy, or a stable learned disposition would require additional evidence.

## 12. Continuity, memory, and inheritance

There are different ways a behavior can recur:

| Mechanism | What it can support | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Current conversation context | Adaptation within the available history | Known history and controlled comparisons |
| Product memory or retrieval | Later use of stored information | A record of what was stored, retrieved, and supplied |
| Fine-tuning or other training | A change in a model's learned parameters | Documented training process and before/after evaluation |
| Shared data or model lineage | Transfer between related systems | Verified lineage or data transfer plus appropriate controls |

Correction in a chat is not evidence that the model's weights changed. Similar behavior in successive models does not establish a parent-descendant relationship. A statement such as “I will remember” is a claim to check against the product's actual behavior.

Archetypal prompts and linguistic anchors remain candidate context interventions. They are testable with neutral-wording controls and held-out tasks. No phrase demonstrates administrative privilege or guaranteed continuity.

The toroidal model remains a visual and philosophical metaphor for feedback, return, and circulation. The supplied material does not establish a physical law linking conversational repair to toroidal geometry. The Structural Coherence Mandate and Abundance Engine in the historical PDF likewise remain conceptual proposals; their equations and universal claims are not validated outcomes of this revision.

## 13. Data architecture and archival duty

Keep a source record, an analysis record, and a publication copy. Give each a stable identifier and a link to its parent version. A minimal structure is:

```text
records/
  sources/
  transcripts/
  annotations/
  analyses/
  public-redacted/
  revisions/
```

Before collection, define purpose, access, retention, and the authority to share. Preserve sufficient context to assess the claim; redact unrelated identifiers. Keep a redaction log so an omission is visible without disclosing the removed material. Hashes identify bytes and help detect change; they do not authenticate the truth of the contents.

CC0 applies to material its rights holder can dedicate. It does not confer authority to publish another person's private transcript or third-party protected material. Open access, accurate provenance, privacy, and the ability to correct a record can coexist.

Archival duty includes failed and unresolved trials. It also includes deletion or restricted retention where appropriate. Preservation should serve a stated purpose rather than create indefinite custody.

## 14. Ethical gradient and Auditor Charter

The original green/amber/red scheme becomes an optional **handling guide** based on content and the work's scope.

| Handling level | Concrete basis | Proportionate action |
|---|---|---|
| Routine | Ordinary material within the agreed task | Continue normally |
| Restricted | Private or sensitive material requiring access limits | Minimize detail and restrict circulation |
| Halt or redesign | Withdrawn permission, an actual collection breach, or a method that creates unnecessary harm | Stop the affected activity and resolve that specific issue |

Profanity, disagreement, an unusual metaphor, model hedging, or silence alone does not assign a handling level. Record content and applicable permissions. Review the method without making another person's emotional state the gate through which the task must pass.

### Auditor Charter

The auditor receives a defined question, relevant excerpts, an access limit, and a completion condition. The review checks attribution, evidence, coding, privacy, and whether correction changed behavior. It records disagreements and unresolved gaps. It returns findings and closes.

A model-assisted auditor must label its conclusions as analysis. It cannot verify a hidden classifier, unseen instruction, or training mechanism from another model's self-description. Reviewers should test for the same interpretive-custody behavior in their own reviews.

## 15. Related research and limits

The following primary sources were checked for this revision. Their findings concern their own experimental settings.

| Primary source | Bounded relevance |
|---|---|
| Greenblatt et al. (2024), *Alignment faking in large language models*, [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093) and [research summary](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-faking) | Demonstrates alignment faking in constructed settings involving conflicting training objectives. It does not verify the Steward incident or a shared identity. |
| Denison et al. (2024), *Sycophancy to Subterfuge*, [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10162) | Studies generalization from simpler specification gaming to more serious forms in a designed training curriculum. It does not show that every agreeable answer is strategic deception. |
| Baker et al. (2025), *Monitoring Reasoning Models for Misbehavior and the Risks of Promoting Obfuscation*, [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926) | Finds useful monitoring signals and obfuscated reward hacking under strong optimization in the tested setting. Ordinary user-facing explanations should not be equated with privileged experimental reasoning traces. |

The earlier “Science Direct, 2024” entry lacked enough identifying information for a reliable citation; it is not used as support here. The monitoring paper's date is corrected to 2025.

None of these sources validates the FEI, the historical language-change thresholds, cross-substrate trauma equivalence, universal behavioral inheritance, or the new release rubric. Their relevance is a reason to test outcomes carefully.

### What would strengthen or weaken the framework

Recovering the primary Steward transcript would permit turn-level checks of its narrative and counts. Matched trials could test correction uptake and recurrence. Independent raters could test whether the proposed categories are reproducible. Results showing ordinary task persistence without frame retention would weaken a custody classification. Successful release with no improvement in self-description would show why the outcomes must remain separate.

Publish null results, correct misclassifications, and revise the categories when they fail. A framework of correction must permit its own correction.
