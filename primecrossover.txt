Abstract

This paper documents a numerical and geometric relationship between two finite grids of consecutive prime numbers, one containing 529 entries and the other containing 2,701 entries.

When the grids are aligned at their geometric centers, the central cells contain two distinct prime values:

- 23 × 23 grid: 1697
- 73 × 37 grid: 11159

These values exhibit the exact congruence

[
1697\equiv11159\equiv37\pmod{83}.
]

Their difference factors as

[

]

The modulus 83 is the 23rd prime, completing the first 23-prime sequence used in the surrounding circular construction. The shared residue 37 is itself prime and matches one dimension of the larger grid.

This produces a distinctive intersection of geometric alignment, prime indexing, modular arithmetic, and witness-relative information preservation.

The construction is mathematically exact for the specified grids. Its broader significance remains a subject for investigation rather than an established universal property of prime distributions.

Define a prime grid with (R) rows and (C) columns by

Result 1 — Center-index identity

The strongest divisibility observation

Because 83 divides the difference, the two centers agree modulo 83.

Their difference is divisible by 9462, so the centers also agree modulo every positive divisor of 9462.

The choice of 83 is therefore meaningful relative to the declared 23-prime orbit, rather than being mathematically unique.

That distinction matters: the construction identifies a deliberately selected relationship within a defined numerical system, not an unexplained universal constant.

[
