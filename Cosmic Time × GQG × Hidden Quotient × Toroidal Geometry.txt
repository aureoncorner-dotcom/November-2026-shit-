Core result: three seven-edge families
Number seven cyclic slots i=0,...,6 in order around the solar ecliptic circle. The complete graph has C(7,2)=21 distinct unordered edges. The cyclic relabeling i -> i+1 (mod 7) partitions these edges into exactly three orbits:
* Orbit I, step 1: {i,i+1}, seven edges; polygon boundary C7.
* Orbit II, step 2: {i,i+2}, seven edges; star polygon {7/2}.
* Orbit III, step 3: {i,i+3}, seven edges; star polygon {7/3}.
For an unordered pair, steps 4, 5, 6 equal steps 3, 2, 1 in the reverse direction. Each family traverses all seven vertices because gcd(7,k)=1 for k=1,2,3. Their edge sets are pairwise disjoint and cover all 21 edges; this was checked exhaustively in the companion code.
This gives an exact 3×7 structural decomposition. The three families describe angular-rank slots, not three fixed lists of planet identities. At a different time the planets occupying the seven slots can change. Nothing in this graph theorem establishes a physical cosmic influence.
Phase geometry and where information disappears
For seven observed heliocentric longitude phases, the full angular state is (S¹)^7. Removing one common rotation yields a six-dimensional phase torus (S¹)^6. Define sorted longitudes λ_i, cyclic gaps g_i=(λ_(i+1)-λ_i) mod 360°, and d=360°/7=51.428571...°.
A regular angular heptagon has g_i=d for all slots. The seven gap errors e_i=g_i-d sum to zero, leaving only six independent gap coordinates. Define chord-family errors
F_k(t) = sqrt((1/7) sum_i [((λ_(i+k)-λ_i) mod 360°) - k*d]^2), k=1,2,3.
These are NOT independent physical measurements. The step-two errors are e_i+e_(i+1) and step-three errors are e_i+e_(i+1)+e_(i+2) (cyclic indices). All three families are transformations of the same six relative-phase degrees of freedom. Multiplying apparent features does not multiply independent evidence.
The sevenfold Fourier magnitude R7=|(1/7)Σexp(7iλ_j)| suffers a provable witness collision:
* All seven bodies on exactly the same ray: R7=1 and R1=1.
* Perfect sevenfold spacing: R7=1 and R1=0.
Thus R7 alone does not determine the witness R1. In GQG notation, N_(R1)(R7) is nonempty. Retain both, and preferably the labeled longitudes and cyclic gaps. Even the pair (R1,R7) loses handedness under λ -> -λ, since both magnitudes are unchanged; a signed directed witness changes sign. No untested global sufficiency claim is made for any chosen reduced record.

	Reading the table. Full-search estimates use (b+1)/(999+1), where b is the number of surrogate minima at least as favorable (F1 no larger) as the candidate's daily score. The baseline is a raw single-instant fraction, with a different sampling question and threshold. For 2003 the 95% Monte Carlo intervals are 0.561–0.623, 0.552–0.614 and 0.562–0.624 in table order. The 999/999 entries have intervals 0.996–1.000; 1.000 does not prove an underlying probability of exactly one. These intervals concern simulated realizations, not independent orbital days; they are not simultaneous intervals. Resolution is 0.001.
Descriptive rarity versus significance [A §§2–4]. The actual daily scan has 1, 56 and 9 dates at least as good as the respective 2003, 2029 and 2084 daily scores, out of 109,573. These are within-window ranks in a correlated history, not astronomical probabilities. The 2003 event is the strongest daily configuration in this reconstruction, yet about 58%–59% of full surrogate searches do at least as well. The source-time refinement check also gives non-small 2003 tails (0.547, 0.529, 0.534), but favors the observed candidate with finer timing than the controls and is not a matched continuous-time test. Search-adjusted fixed-membership duration tests likewise provide no support for exceptional candidate duration; the post-inspection 10° threshold has no privileged confirmatory status.
What the surrogates preserve and change [A §3]. All-body rotations independently add one constant angle to each body's complete track, preserving each body's rate history while randomizing interbody orientation. Epoch offsets shift each body by an independently selected integer number of days in stated ranges, evaluating the ephemeris continuously beyond the interval without wrap seams; this preserves orbital-rate variation but is an analyst-defined construction, not an exact permutation group. Inner rotations randomize Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars while retaining Jupiter through Pluto, including the actual Neptune–Pluto phase history.
Limits [A §§1,3,6,8]. These are conditional reference distributions, not dynamically integrated alternative Solar Systems. The first two disrupt actual interplanetary relationships; the third still alters inner/outer relations. The original runner's objective, exact cadence/endpoints, software version and refinement details were not fully recovered; undocumented prior search choices cannot be corrected from unavailable history. The declared date-and-subset search is included, without assuming independent days. The result supports neither a universal physical probability nor proof that every possible physical hypothesis is false.
