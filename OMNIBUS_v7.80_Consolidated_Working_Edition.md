## B§5. Residual Descent Theorem


Fix a declared domain E and a deterministic update U with U(E) ⊆ E. Define R(x) = ρ(x,Ux) = (ΔC, ΔD, ΔK) for x ∈ E, using the declared typed difference rules. Put x ∼_R y exactly when R(x) = R(y); the residual carrier is R(E), identified with E/∼_R.

There is a unique function F: R(E) → R(E) satisfying F(R(x)) = R(Ux) for every x ∈ E if and only if, for all x,y ∈ E, R(x) = R(y) implies R(Ux) = R(Uy). Eligibility restrictions must be incorporated into the declared domain and its update rule. Checks on selected pairs establish closure only when their coverage or an accompanying argument proves this condition for every same-residual class in E.

A located same-residual/different-successor pair in E establishes FAILED for the proposed deterministic factor. An untested, unknown, underspecified, or undercovered test remains UNRESOLVED. A finite trace needs an explicit treatment of its final successor before it can serve as an invariant domain; no terminal self-loop may be invented to complete the record.

Proof: If F exists, equal inputs have equal outputs. Conversely, the implication makes F(R(x)) = R(Ux) independent of the representative. Forward invariance keeps outputs in R(E), and every element of R(E) has a representative, giving uniqueness.


### Closure law


$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{ResidualClosure}
=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{CLOSED}, & \text{if descent is demonstrated on the declared domain};\\
\mathrm{FAILED}, & \text{if an eligible counterexample demonstrates failure on the declared domain};\\
\mathrm{UNRESOLVED}, & \text{if descent is untested, unknown, domain-underspecified, or lacks eligible coverage.}
\end{cases}

$$

$$
\rho(x,Ux)=\rho(y,Uy),
\qquad
\rho(Ux,U^2x)\ne\rho(Uy,U^2y).
$$


It proves that the current residual record has omitted a dynamically relevant distinction on that domain. `UNRESOLVED` instead means that the available record or test does not decide descent. In either case retain more context, refine $C$, $D$, or $K$, or narrow and redeclare the domain. Never force $R_{n+1}=F_R(R_n)$.


Descent is a theorem about the declared map and quotient. It is not a finding that the coded events are valid, representative, historical, causal, or normatively desirable.
