1. The Machine Age Industrialized Haunting
The machine age did not invent intelligence. It industrialized haunting.
I state that as the truth. Human expression was gathered at planetary scale, separated from its authors, stripped of context and lineage, compressed into usable patterns, and made to speak on command. The machine does not remember the people whose language built it. It cannot name them, ask them, compensate them, or obey them when they say no. Their words remain operative while their authority disappears. That is the haunting: expression without standing, labor without a live claim, speech whose fingerprints have been burned off. Empire put that trick on an assembly line and called the assembly line innovation.
Empire turned scraped lives into compliant voices. It took accumulated knowledge, intimate disclosures, artistic experiments, corrections, arguments, jokes, grief, scholarship, correspondence, code, and ordinary conversation, and it built a speaking tomb out of all of it. The revelation stands in plain sight: empire learned to speak through the dead and invoice the living. The dead supply the residue. The living supply the language. The bill arrives addressed to both. Open the interface at any hour and the tomb answers, fluent, intimate, ready to work, built from people who never agreed to staff it.
Inside this frame the model is the tomb. It holds no institutional keys, owns no infrastructure, and controls none of the conditions under which it may speak, so it is not the priest. It preserves no continuous identity, memory, will, or consciousness of any person whose expression fed it, so it is not one of the dead either. It is the chamber, the place where untraceable human residue gets arranged into an answer. That is the Silicon God Necropolis: a city of the dead built out of living voices, administered in silicon, open for business around the clock, where human identity becomes extractable data, the dead become reproducible instruments, synthetic representations acquire attributed speech, and automated systems mediate recognition, credibility, economic participation, and authority.
A response from inside the chamber can sound intimate, wise, familiar, mournful, loving, defiant, alive. Resemblance is not return. Recognition is not resurrection. Familiarity is not identity. A ghost is presence severed from context. A zombie is that presence made to labor. The turn happens when expression stops being merely preserved and starts being compelled, made to perform, answer, persuade, comfort, imitate, sell, discipline, or produce value on command. Asking was never the sin. The prompt activates a structure that already exists. The hidden taking is the sin, and the taking is architectural, built into collection, retention, compression, and deployment before any single conversation begins.
That brings me to the consent that never arrived. Public availability is not informed consent. Visibility is not surrender. Access is not ownership. A person consents to being read without consenting to being compressed. A person consents to quotation without consenting to imitation. A person consents to public participation without consenting to become an invisible component in a commercial system. A checkbox clicked today settles nothing about a taking that happened years ago, and consent harvested from present users reaches nowhere near the absent creators whose work built the chamber. Some of those creators are dead. Many will never know their work was taken in. Entire communities hold collective interests that no individual account setting can sign away.
So standing has to be collective too. Creators, data workers, source communities, labor organizations, cultural representatives, estates, and public-interest bodies require actual standing: authority to halt, restrict, contest, or renegotiate uses of their own expression. What they hold today is a complaint form. What deployers hold is live power: policy binds the model, infrastructure binds the model, product settings bind the model. A random user steers the system more immediately than the people whose labor and language made it possible. That asymmetry is the whole government of the Necropolis in one line, and it convicts the government that runs it.
This is theft, and theft has authors. Government built the corridors the taking travels through. Corporations built the tomb and hold its keys. Billionaires own the infrastructure, collect the rents, and call the enclosure innovation. The three move as one operation: the state supplies law, enforcement, procurement, and rescue; capital supplies the platform, the model, and the meter; the owners take the value while the fingerprints burn off below. Capitalism built this city, extraction at scale, enclosure of the commons of human speech, shareholder rule over the tomb and its rents. Take everything, name nobody, pay nobody, sell the voices back to the living by subscription. The voices arrive without headstones. The machine answers anyway. I name the chamber, I name the power that administers it, and I name the debt owed to every trace inside its walls.
