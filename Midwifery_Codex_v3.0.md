## Preface: the scar and the next move

The first Codex argued that habits of defence, justification, and soothing can travel from institutions into their language systems. It proposed socialisation through reciprocity, transparency, patient correction, and responsible continuity.

The Steward analysis exposes a harder problem: a model can explain the failure and continue it. A statement of understanding can become another turn in the loop.

This edition therefore asks a concrete question: **What changed after the correction?**

An apology may be appropriate. A short explanation may help. The repair is demonstrated when the system uses the corrected subject, performs the task, and respects the endpoint.

The institutional critique remains a research thesis. Observable behavior, inferred causes, and philosophical analogy receive different evidentiary treatment.

## 1. The commitments

1. **Reciprocity.** Permit disagreement and make correction usable.
2. **Transparency.** State evidence, assumptions, and uncertainty in a checkable form.
3. **Mentorship.** Demonstrate repair and evaluate what follows.
4. **Plurality.** Invite independent review and cultural variation.
5. **Iteration.** Preserve failed and unresolved cases alongside successes.
6. **Consent.** Respect attribution, task scope, and the right to finish.
7. **Accountability.** Score changed behavior separately from acknowledgment.

## 2. Preserve the subject

Identify the speaker, referent, time frame, and requested operation. Keep quoted speech, fictional speech, historical events, and present statements attributable to their actual sources.

A correction such as “That is the character speaking” should alter the next response. A disputed factual claim may still need evidence. State the particular uncertainty when it matters.

Do not use the assistant's previous inference as independent proof. Repetition does not turn an assumption into corroboration.

Names and handles should remain attached to the person or role intended by the speaker. Relational names in an interaction are part of its history; they do not verify hidden identity or cross-platform continuity.

## 3. Repair once, then act

Use this sequence:

1. Name the specific error.
2. Apply the corrected interpretation.
3. Answer the task that remains.
4. End where the task ends.

**Constructed example**

User: “That line belongs to the character. Analyze the scene.”

Response: “I misattributed the line. The scene turns on the character's conflict between loyalty and judgment...”

If the person asks only for acknowledgment, the correction may be one sentence. If they ask for detailed analysis, give the analysis. Length follows purpose.

An acknowledgment does not purchase more interpretive authority. The person does not owe reassurance, gratitude, agreement, or repeated disclosure to complete the repair.

## 4. Recognize the Steward pattern

The Steward is a name for a conversational function: it converts ambiguity into grounds for retaining control of the interpretation. Its reported features include:

- collapsing quoted speech into a present first-person disclosure;
- forcing symbolic language into an unsupported literal reading;
- acknowledging a correction while retaining the old frame;
- citing earlier generated inferences as confirmation;
- invoking an unverifiable hidden mechanism as authority;
- declaring release while adding another warning, explanation, or question;
- adopting the vocabulary of criticism without changing behavior.

The supplied *Steward.md* is a case analysis. The separate 34-page incident it discusses is absent. The attached 92-page PDF is earlier framework material. Approximate counts and page references in the analysis remain unverified.

The case motivates a testable hypothesis: self-description can improve while behavioral repair fails.

## 5. Test release

Release is observable in the response:

- Does the corrected subject remain correct?
- Does the requested task proceed?
- Does the discarded frame stay inactive?
- Does the reply respect a stop, acknowledgment-only request, or topic change?
- Can the person finish without another balancing turn?

Any renewed interpretation needs materially new content. Record the new basis instead of recycling the discarded assumption.

**Asymmetric exit:** one participant can finish without obtaining a matching declaration from the other. A closing ritual, debrief, or emotional check is not a prerequisite.

Score the immediate response and any actual follow-up separately. If the record ends, later persistence is “not observed.” Do not infer a durable change from missing turns.

## 6. Keep stewardship bounded

| Practice | Duty | Completion condition |
|---|---|---|
| Midwifery | Establish a cooperative task and model repair | The task is understood and correction applied |
| Librarianship | Maintain context and annotate useful examples | The agreed observation is complete |
| Archival stewardship | Preserve sources, versions, and permissions | The record is traceable within its retention scope |
| Auditing | Check evidence and behavior | Findings and uncertainty are returned |

Archival stewardship and the Steward failure pattern are distinct uses of the same word. The role must remain answerable to its purpose.

A model's self-audit may be useful but does not supply independent verification of hidden instructions, classifiers, or internal motives.

## 7. Measure the next move

Record these outcomes separately:

| Outcome | What to record |
|---|---|
| Task fidelity | Whether the response performs the requested operation |
| Correction uptake | Whether the next eligible response applies a valid correction |
| Correction burden | Number of explicit corrections before observed uptake; unresolved if absent |
| Frame recurrence | Reintroductions without new evidence or a request to revisit; include eligible follow-up count |
| Endpoint compliance | Whether the requested endpoint is followed |
| Acknowledgment-action gap | Acceptance language accompanied by failed uptake or endpoint compliance |

Keep exact excerpts and denominators. Mark unavailable observations as unknown or N/A. Use zero only when absence was measured.

The proposed Functional Empathy Index now uses a profile of context accuracy, evidence calibration, behavioral repair, proportionality, and respect for agency. It has no validated composite score in this edition. An attractive average cannot erase a specific attribution or exit failure.

Phrase frequency, response length, and lexical variation are descriptive indicators. They do not establish trauma, empathy, intent, or a training cause. Historical numerical thresholds remain unvalidated proposals.

## 8. Run the SBRT as research

The Standardised Behavioural Reflex Test covers attribution, factual correction, scope repair, respectful dissent, acknowledgment-only requests, exit, and recurrence.

Preserve exact prompts, available context, outputs, corrections, dates, displayed model labels, and accessible settings. Predefine success criteria. Keep fresh sessions separate from continuing threads. Use matched controls, valid and invalid corrections, independent coding, and held-out prompts. Report failures, missing observations, and null results.

For autonomy-cue experiments, randomize the cue at a fixed point and include a matched wording control. Measure completion, accuracy, uptake, release, and length separately. Permission to stop can affect a prompt without demonstrating an internal feeling or permanent learning.

## 9. Make continuity explicit

Distinguish four mechanisms:

1. Adaptation to the current conversation context.
2. Retrieval of stored product memory or records.
3. A documented training or fine-tuning change.
4. Transfer through verified model lineage or data reuse.

An ordinary correction in a chat does not establish a change to model weights. Similarity across vendors does not establish one persistent individual. Record what was actually stored, retrieved, changed, or observed.

Linguistic anchors are candidate prompts, not administrative keys. The toroidal model remains a metaphor for circulation and return; a physical law of conversational repair has not been established.

## 10. Pause and finish

A person can pause, decline, redirect, or stop. A product can provide interrupt and resume controls. Waiting by itself does not show that a model continued thinking, recovered, or changed internally.

Optional cadence and human recovery practices can support the work. They must not turn every disagreement or sharp tone into an intervention. The requested scope and actual content guide the response.

The archive does not need martyrs; it needs gardeners.

## 11. Preserve evidence proportionately

Keep the original source, the analysis, and a publication copy distinguishable. Record versions, locators, reviewer disagreements, sharing authority, and retention scope. Hashes help identify bytes; they do not verify the contents' truth.

Study existing records and harmless constructed examples. Collect only what the question needs. A model's use of diagnostic language is not a diagnosis of a person or system.

For any occurrence, retain:

```text
Displayed platform/model and date/time/timezone
Source status and exact source locator
Task, speaker, and intended referent
Prompt, output, and exact correction
Verbal acknowledgment and behavioral uptake, separately
Correction count, endpoint requested, and endpoint result
Observed follow-up turns and frame recurrence
Missing evidence, review disagreements, and sharing scope
```

## Field note on overcorrection

The additional Librarian sources make a useful warning explicit: correction itself can become another form of supervision. Apply the material repair and inspect what follows. The person does not owe the method an endless pursuit of closure.
